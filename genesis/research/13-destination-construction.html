Director-derived destination construction

The camera-derived destination approach was motivated by Terran's technique of using an existing frame plus camera/location movement instructions to derive a subsequent viewpoint. The agentic Director, trusted-media runtime, and FLUX Kontext Pro construction path recorded here are current research, not Terran's implementation.

This chapter records the first end-to-end product evidence for: filmmaker image and story → visually grounded Director plan → image-conditioned destination construction → an actual generated next set. One destination only. Cinematographer review, Camotion, and video were not run. Camotion Phase 1 remains frozen.

Director decides where to go. Destination construction creates the next set.

The product path is: Project A (trusted runtime media) → Director plans B → a deterministic application-layer prompt from B's spatial intent and visual description → existing ImageEditProvider.editImage → FLUX Kontext Pro → generated still → trusted runtime media identity → the same planned B becomes actual B. The Director is not rerun. A, story, and C…N stay as planned. Constructed B is not a Cinematographer-approved canonical, not a Camotion input, and not evidence that A→B is shootable.

Fixture-independent Director plan

VISUALLY GROUNDED

Authoritative starting frame: filmmaker-uploaded alien salt-flat with a distant black rock monolith and a bright vertical fissure. The Director request contained the filmmaker story, that uploaded runtime startMediaId, and no stale Wardrobe startFrameIntent.

Filmmaker story:

Travel forward into this world and discover what lies beyond the apparent path ahead. The journey should remain physically continuous and increasingly strange, passing through several distinct spaces that feel like parts of the same world. Eventually discover something completely unexpected.

Summary: A descent through the glowing monolith fissure into an unexpected subterranean high-tech archive.

  • B — Move forward through the bright fissure into the narrow canyon interior. A tight, vertical stone corridor illuminated by intense, molten light seeping from the rock walls.
  • C — Follow a magma vein downward into a dark cave entrance at the canyon's base. A jagged underground tunnel where glowing magma forms stepping stones surrounded by heavy steam.
  • D — Emerge from the tunnel into a vast, enclosed cavern. A massive cavern overgrown with giant, bioluminescent crystalline trees and glowing metallic ferns.
  • E — Pass through a perfectly circular, artificial metallic doorway hidden behind the flora. A pristine, brightly lit futuristic server room, completely sterile and contrasting the natural caves.
Planning-level visual grounding, not physical shootability.

The Director visibly grounded on the uploaded A. The monolith/fissure interpretation came from the image rather than from filmmaker story text. The plan expresses a spatial sequence using traversable transitions and new volumes. That validates planning-level visual grounding and fixture independence. It does not validate physical shootability of B→C→D→E. B→E remain Director plans until actual sets are constructed. Cinematographer inspection of generated sets has not happened for this route.

Constructed B is not shootable evidence. Spatial continuity of the route is not completed.

Observation / hypothesis, not a conclusion.

The generated image may represent an intermediate spatial position X: A (salt flat / distant fissure) → X (at or just inside the fissure) → requested B (narrow interior corridor). That suggests a future research question: can large Director-planned spatial moves require intermediate derived destinations to preserve both world continuity and meaningful camera displacement? Continuity and displacement may be competing objectives during image-conditioned destination construction. These are hypotheses from one run, not established fact. Do not implement automatic intermediate-frame generation from this observation. Do not change the Director or the destination data model from this result.
