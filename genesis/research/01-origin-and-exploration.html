TUNNELVISION · PROTOTYPE STORY + EXPERIMENT LOG

TunnelVision

From Terran Boylan's original TunnelVision technique to agentic filmmaking research: the origin, hypotheses, experiments, failures, and architecture.

Working principle A good next frame should preserve the world’s rules while changing the viewer’s question.
ORIGIN + PROBLEM

Terran's TunnelVision

TunnelVision originated with filmmaker Terran Boylan, who developed the concept and manual filmmaking technique through work for his own YouTube channel and video content. That work predates the Peter Gabriel Digging in the Dirt project. The technique was later adapted for that film, where we used the playful name TunnelTV, an homage to MTV. By iteratively generating successive viewpoints, selecting the next frame, and carrying that image forward as the new visual reference, Terran's original workflow created the feeling of continuously traveling through an imagined environment.

The research documented here extends Terran's original TunnelVision work into an agentic filmmaking system: an AI Director decides where the journey should go, and an AI Cinematographer determines how to move the camera there. The ambition expands from a manually authored sequence into continuous exploration of potentially endless imagined worlds.

Generative models are good at:
making compelling individual images and clips.
The harder problem is:
maintaining a meaningful journey through an imagined world.
TunnelVision explores:
direction, continuity, preference learning, and traversability.
Working principle A good next frame should preserve the world's rules while changing the viewer's question.
OBSERVE→PROPOSE→GENERATE→EVALUATE→SELECT→MOVE

The manual experiments below intentionally reproduce this loop by hand. The goal of the coded prototype is to turn those repeated decisions into an agent workflow.

STILL-IMAGE EXPLORATION

Teaching the journey what “forward” means

Using Nano Banana Pro through Runway, each selected frame became the reference for the next generation. Candidate selection was treated as data: spatial progression, aesthetic preference, emergent discoveries, and rejected compositions all shaped the next prompt.

CANONICAL FRAME NOTATION
Only frames selected into the continuing journey receive letters. The starting frame is A; subsequent selected frames are B–H. Rejected and unselected candidates remain unlettered. Traversal tests can therefore be referenced simply as A → B, B → C, and so on.
START

Starting image

No map, storyboard, destination, mushrooms, chasm, bridge, or giant tree was planned in advance. The experiment began with a single first-person view from beneath the earth.

01
ITERATION INTENT

Cross the threshold

Reference entering this iteration

Prompt

First-person point-of-view shot, continuing forward from inside the earthen root-tunnel through the bright opening on the left and emerging outside into open air. The camera has passed beyond the threshold of the underground passage and now looks straight ahead across the landscape beyond it. The foreground at the very edges of frame still shows the rough lip of the earthen opening - eroded, ochre-and-terracotta soil with a few thin tree roots dangling into frame from the top edge - grounding the shot as a true continuation of the same tunnel exit. Beyond the opening, reveal a sunlit exterior landscape whose geology matches the tunnel's material logic: eroded sedimentary earth banks, exposed root systems from the trees above, and dry compacted soil, now overgrown with the trees whose roots were seen hanging inside. Daylight is bright, slightly cool white-blue, coming from a high sun, in strong contrast to the warm golden-orange interior tones left behind, with the transition of light logically brighter and cooler the further from the tunnel mouth. Natural depth: dappled sunlight filtering through a canopy of trees whose roots plunge into the eroded earth walls, tall grass and undergrowth on the forest floor, a soft haze in the distance. The perspective remains eye-level, first-person, looking forward along the same sightline as if a single continuous walk carried the viewer from underground into daylight.

Exact prompt used in the prototype.

Generated results

Result: The camera crossed the threshold, but the cave remained visible
The prompt successfully carried the viewer through the opening, but it intentionally left the lip of the cave at the frame edges. That made the next problem obvious: to create a genuinely new location, we needed to move far enough forward that the previous location was completely behind the camera.
PROMPT / LOGIC CHANGE

Next intent: force true spatial advancement. The new prompt explicitly placed the camera roughly 20 feet outside and required the tunnel entrance to be completely out of view, behind the camera.

Reference entering this iteration

Prompt

The trees whose roots were seen dangling inside the tunnel now stand fully visible, tall and mature, their root systems clawing into the eroded earth around their bases. A gentle haze softens the distance. Eye-level, first-person perspective, camera looking straight ahead, no trace of the tunnel entrance anywhere in frame.

Exact prompt used to create the first fully exterior frame. That successful frame was then used to create three additional variations.

Generated results

Selected: Forest ravine with eroded walls and exposed roots
The fully exterior frame solved the displacement problem. We then used it as the reference for three additional variations and selected Candidate 3 — the forest ravine with eroded walls and exposed roots — because it preserved the geology of the tunnel while establishing a strong, naturally navigable route deeper into the world.
PROMPT / LOGIC CHANGE

Key criterion established here: a selected frame must represent a meaningfully new camera position, not merely a changed view from the old one. From here, the existing sequence and Candidate 3 selection continue as documented.

SEQUENCE-LEVEL REVIEW

The reel exposed a failure we missed while judging frames one at a time.

Clicking through the canonical journey changed the diagnosis. Several selected frames preserved the world and advanced its aesthetic, but did not convincingly encode a new camera position. The environment evolved while the viewpoint stayed nearly stationary.

B → C

Feels less like forward travel than a re-dressing of the same viewpoint: more roots and enclosure, but little unmistakable camera displacement.

D → E

The world becomes more ominous and architectural, but the camera again appears to remain in nearly the same place.

F → G

The scene changes dramatically, yet the new geography can read as transformation around a stationary camera rather than a location reached by traversal.

New Director criterion: perceptible camera displacement

A candidate should not count as spatial advancement merely because the scene is different. It should provide visual evidence that the camera itself moved: foreground landmarks passed behind it, occlusion relationships changed, perspective/parallax shifted, or genuinely new geography became visible.

The reel therefore becomes more than presentation. A TunnelVision agent can keep the growing canonical sequence as working visual memory, periodically re-evaluate local transitions and the global trajectory, and repair frames that preserve style but fail to communicate movement.
