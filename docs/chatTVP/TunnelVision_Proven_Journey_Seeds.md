# TunnelVision --- Proven Journey Seeds & Active-World Patterns

These are examples of human / filmmaker-level Story Ideas that have
produced useful TunnelVision journeys or represent refinements of
successful experiments.

They are not production prompts and should not be copied mechanically.

They demonstrate the level of intent a filmmaker should be able to
provide before Prompt Coach / Screenwriter conditioning.

## The Two Axes of a Strong TunnelVision Journey

Successful TunnelVision journeys tend to combine two different kinds of
motion:

**SPATIAL PROGRESSION + TEMPORAL VITALITY**

Or more simply:

**CAMERA MOTION + WORLD MOTION**

### Spatial Progression

The camera physically travels through connected space.

Useful human-level journey language includes:

-   through the doorway
-   down the stairs
-   around the corner
-   along the road
-   into the tunnel
-   across the platform
-   through the waterfall
-   out the window

Destinations should represent meaningful progress through the world
rather than merely changing the contents of a static composition.

### Temporal Vitality

The world should usually be doing something while the camera travels.

A journey through beautiful but motionless environments can produce
technically successful spatial progression while still feeling visually
dead.

Useful activity can come from people, persistent characters, animals and
creatures, vehicles, crowds, machinery, weather, water, vegetation, fire
and sparks, debris, environmental reactions, physical events, or surreal
activity.

Favor active verbs such as:

**racing, climbing, chasing, scattering, reaching, swarming, turning,
falling, crossing, dancing, swimming, erupting, collapsing, blowing,
reacting, firing, flowing.**

Compare:

> There are cars at the intersection.

with:

> Cars stream through the intersection and slam on their brakes as we
> race across.

Compare:

> An abandoned subway platform.

with:

> A nearly empty train roars past while loose paper blows along the
> platform and distant maintenance workers move beneath flickering
> lights.

Compare:

> A decaying ballroom filled with ghosts.

with:

> Translucent dancers continue spinning through the decaying ballroom
> while curtains billow and broken chandeliers sway overhead.

## Activity Does Not Mean "Add People"

Do not populate every journey with characters simply to create motion.

A quiet or abandoned environment can remain lonely while still feeling
alive:

-   trees bend in the wind
-   rainwater runs down stairs
-   birds scatter from rooftops
-   loose curtains move through broken windows
-   dust blows through sunlight
-   machinery turns somewhere underground
-   fog rolls across the floor
-   blood cells rush through vessels
-   neurons fire around the camera
-   fish scatter through underwater ruins

Activity should belong naturally to the world and tone.

## Spatial Progression Still Comes First

World activity must not become a substitute for camera travel.

Changing weather, characters, lighting, activity, objects, or visual
style while leaving the camera effectively in the same place is not a
successful TunnelVision journey.

The preferred hierarchy is:

1.  Camera travels through physically connected space.
2.  The world feels alive while the camera travels.
3.  Activity supports the story rather than becoming detailed
    choreography.

## Human / FM Voice

Story Ideas should still sound like something a filmmaker might quickly
type.

Good:

> POV racing downhill through a crowded city, cars crossing the
> intersections and people jumping out of the way as we keep picking up
> speed.

Not necessary:

> At 2.4 seconds a sedan crosses camera-right while three pedestrians
> accelerate laterally toward the sidewalk.

The filmmaker describes the journey and its energy. TunnelVision handles
the filmmaking.

## Persistent Subjects

Moving subjects can give a journey particularly strong temporal
propulsion.

Examples include a squirrel, mouse, paper airplane, skier, car, pram,
toy racer, animal, or vehicle.

The subject can function as a visual guide that continuously pulls the
camera deeper into the world.

This does not automatically require FOLLOW grammar. For example:

> POV chasing a red paper airplane just ahead of us

can preserve unembodied POV while giving the camera a persistent moving
target.

## FOLLOW Semantics

Experiments suggest that generic FOLLOW and explicit human relationship
language can behave differently.

For fast-moving subjects:

> FOLLOW directly behind...

has sometimes produced stronger forward-travel behavior than a generic
FOLLOW instruction.

Generic FOLLOW may encourage an attractive but relatively stable rear
three-quarter hero composition.

Preserve explicit filmmaker language such as **directly behind**, **just
ahead of us**, **alongside**, **chasing**, **gaining distance**, or
**pulling away** when supplied.

Do not automatically invent a detailed camera taxonomy from these
phrases.

## Duration and Hop Count

Overall duration is filmmaker intent:

> around 30 seconds\
> around 45 seconds\
> around 1 minute

Preserve it as approximate journey scope. Do not translate it into
individual traversal timing.

Likewise, explicit requests such as:

> 5 hops\
> 6 hops\
> 8 destinations

may be intentional creative or product constraints. Preserve them rather
than deciding the filmmaker probably needs fewer.

## Escalation

A strong Story Idea often describes how activity evolves rather than
choreographing every event.

Useful filmmaker language includes:

-   increasingly dangerous
-   stranger as we go
-   more crowded and chaotic
-   darker and wetter
-   keep picking up speed
-   escalate the spectacle
-   gradually become calm
-   increasingly surreal

This gives Director room to create destinations while giving the
generated movie an overall temporal arc.

## Endings

A clear ending can give the entire journey purpose:

-   the tracks simply stop
-   return to the original station
-   emerge behind the waterfall
-   reach the treasure chamber
-   land gently on the steps
-   arrive at the glowing city

An intentionally open ending is also valid:

> build toward a spectacular payoff at the bottom of the hill

when the filmmaker wants Director to invent the resolution.

# Proven / Experimental Human Journey Seeds

## SUBWAY

POV starting in the ticket hall of an almost empty subway station after
closing and keep going deeper. a few last commuters hurry toward the
exits while a worker starts pulling down a security gate. go through the
unpaid side and down a really long escalator while distant people and
maintenance workers move on the levels below. reach the tiled platform
just as a nearly empty train roars through and disappears into the
tunnel, with loose paper blowing in its wake. continue through a service
door into wetter, darker tunnels where water drips from pipes,
ventilation fans turn, cables sway and rats scatter away from us. keep
going until the finished station gives way to raw concrete and
construction equipment, ending in a huge unfinished underground cavern
where work lights flicker, machinery still moves somewhere in the
darkness and the tracks simply stop.

## PIP --- SQUIRREL

FOLLOW a squirrel and keep the same squirrel with us for the whole
journey. let it scamper, leap, climb and occasionally stop to look back
before racing ahead again, always pulling us deeper into a world that
keeps changing and getting stranger. other creatures and things in each
environment can react as the squirrel passes through. the environments
can evolve dramatically and surprise us, but the squirrel should stay
active and recognizable ahead of us throughout the journey.

## ROLLER COASTER

POV riding in the front car of a strange roller coaster, around 1
minute, 8 destinations. keep the track visible ahead of us so it
physically connects the whole journey. take us through wildly different
parts of the ride: elaborate indoor dark rides, outdoor landscapes,
tunnels, caverns, theatrical sets and unexpected architectural spaces.
make every section an active thrill-ride moment with animatronics
lunging and turning, machinery moving, lights flashing, sparks flying,
water blasting, fog rolling across the track, scenery collapsing,
creatures moving around us, swinging objects, near misses, drops,
launches and sharp turns. vary the action so every section has its own
surprise. keep escalating the spectacle and eventually bring the track
all the way back into the original station while ride operators and
waiting passengers react as we arrive, completing the loop.

## WRONG WAY

POV racing way too fast downhill on a narrow steep one-way city street,
going the wrong direction. keep going forward as the street twists hard
left and right through dense city blocks, with sharp corners, dips,
intersections and short straightaways. make it increasingly harrowing:
moving cars cross in front of us, delivery trucks pull into the lane,
cyclists swerve away, pedestrians jump back onto the sidewalk,
construction workers react as barriers topple, and traffic slams on its
brakes at intersections. keep forcing us to steer and change direction
instead of just flying straight ahead. signs, trash and loose objects
whip past in our wake. eventually make it through all the chaos and
emerge into a big open intersection at the bottom of the hill, finally
traveling with the flow of moving traffic in the right direction.

## TUNNELVISION VECTOR

POV through a pure black world made entirely from glowing 1980s arcade
vector graphics. start far away from a huge T-shaped tunnel entrance
floating in darkness, with repeated glowing T-shaped frames pulsing and
disappearing deep inside it. travel into the T as luminous lines streak
past and geometric particles stream around us. keep moving through the
tunnel while its vector framework shifts and pulses until the geometry
gradually becomes a deep V-shaped tunnel made from nested glowing V
frames. race through the V as colored vector shapes, sparks and simple
geometric forms move through the void around us. continue all the way
out into empty black space, where the moving geometry falls away and
becomes still. once we're completely clear of the tunnel, reveal the
complete flat TunnelVision TV mark by itself in the darkness. old Atari
Tempest-style vector graphics, absolute black, glowing cyan, blue,
magenta, yellow, green and red lines, CRT glow and extreme perspective
depth. no realistic surfaces and no photorealism.

## BEEN STILLER NACHT --- MUSEUM

POV starting outside a grand old natural history museum after dark, at
the bottom of its huge stone staircase, while leaves blow across the
steps and lights flicker behind the windows. go inside through the
towering front doors and keep moving deeper as the museum comes alive
around us. ancient animal skeletons begin turning their heads and
stepping down from their displays, paintings shift and their subjects
climb out of the frames, and an enormous deep-sea gallery fills with
impossible creatures swimming through the air while exhibit lights sway.
continue into rooms where artifacts have somehow grown to gigantic scale
and museum objects move on their own. end in the vast central hall where
creatures, figures and exhibits from completely different eras are
moving around and interacting as if the entire museum has awakened.

## HAUNTED VICTORIAN

POV approaching an abandoned Victorian mansion at the edge of a dying
forest on a stormy night. trees thrash in the wind, rain lashes the
gates and loose shutters bang against the house as we make our way
inside. move through a grand foyer where candle flames flicker and
portraits subtly turn their eyes toward us. continue into a dining room
where translucent guests still eat, gesture and raise glasses around a
ghostly dinner. go deeper into a library where books fly from shelves
and objects drift through the air, then into a decaying ballroom where
translucent dancers spin endlessly while curtains billow and a broken
chandelier sways overhead. eventually find a way beneath the house into
a huge hidden chamber where walls flex, dust and stones fall from above,
and something enormous begins moving and awakening in the darkness.

## MIRROR MAZE

POV entering a huge hall of mirrors in an old amusement park after dark.
keep moving deeper while carnival lights pulse outside and reflections
multiply everywhere. at first people and performers move normally
through the maze, but gradually figures start appearing only in
reflections: someone walking behind us who is not really there,
distorted faces moving closer from impossible corridors, shadowy
creatures darting between mirrors and carnival performers who wave or
dance in reflections but vanish whenever we approach. reflections begin
moving independently from the real world as lights flicker and mirrored
panels slowly rotate around us. eventually find the exit and come
completely out into an eerie nighttime midway where rides still turn by
themselves, bulbs chase around old signs, empty swings move in the wind
and strange distant figures wander between the attractions.

## WATERFALL

POV traveling deep into an ancient forest looking for a hidden
waterfall. start beneath huge trees while branches move overhead, birds
scatter through the canopy, insects drift through shafts of sunlight and
streams rush around mossy rocks. keep following the growing sound of
water as vegetation bends in the mist and small animals disappear from
the path ahead. eventually the forest opens and reveals a spectacular
waterfall crashing into a deep teal pool. reach the rocky shoreline and
cross the moving water while fish scatter below us and spray blows
across the surface. approach until the falls fill the view, then pass
directly through the pounding curtain of water into a hidden cave.
continue deeper as underground rivers rush beside us, droplets fall from
enormous ceilings, strange creatures move in dark pools and luminous
mineral formations shimmer in the moving water. end somewhere deep
underground in a vast hidden ecosystem that feels ancient, active and
unexplored.

## FOLDED CITY

POV through a city where gravity gradually stops making sense. begin on
a completely ordinary morning street with pedestrians walking, cyclists
passing and cars moving through intersections. ahead, the road starts
curling upward and traffic somehow continues driving onto the vertical
surface while people grab railings and loose objects tumble in new
directions. keep going into neighborhoods folded at impossible angles,
where people walk on walls and ceilings, buses cross overhead and birds
fly between competing planes of gravity. reach city blocks that have
broken loose and drift above a huge empty gulf while cars, signs and
debris float between them. discover an upside-down city hanging overhead
with trains and traffic moving along its inverted streets. continue
until the two cities almost meet in a huge moving tangle of towers,
streets and floating architecture. end somewhere unexpectedly peaceful:
a little public park suspended between them where children play, leaves
drift through the air and ripples move across a small pond despite the
impossible gravity.

## CHERNOBYL

POV walking quietly through the abandoned Chernobyl Exclusion Zone and
Pripyat like a high-end documentary filmed on location. follow cracked
roads and paths through forests reclaiming the city while trees and tall
grass move in the wind, birds lift from abandoned rooftops and
occasional wildlife crosses the road ahead. move between decaying Soviet
apartment blocks, empty plazas, schools, courtyards and abandoned
buildings where curtains move through broken windows, rainwater drips
through ceilings, doors shift in the breeze and loose paper skitters
across floors. keep exploring through physically connected streets,
doorways and interiors until we eventually pass the abandoned amusement
park, where the rusting Ferris wheel creaks slightly in the wind, and
continue into increasingly overgrown parts of the city. keep everything
restrained and believable: wind, rainwater, dust, broken glass, peeling
paint, rust, damp concrete, wildlife and natural daylight.
photorealistic live-action documentary, not post-apocalyptic fantasy.

## RED PAPER AIRPLANE --- LONG PURSUIT

POV chasing a red paper airplane on a long continuous journey, around 1
minute, 6 hops. start racing just behind it through a busy open-air
market as it darts between moving shoppers, vendors, hanging fabric and
carts being pushed through the crowd. chase it through a narrow doorway
into a dark cluttered workshop where workers turn, machinery moves and
loose papers flutter as the airplane streaks between tables. follow it
out through an open window into a tight city alley where bicycles,
pedestrians, delivery carts and flapping laundry force it to weave
through the space. race after it down into a subway entrance, pursue it
along a crowded underground platform beside a moving train, then follow
it back above ground into the steep streets of San Francisco with
traffic and pedestrians moving around us. the airplane catches an
updraft and climbs toward Nob Hill, gliding between buildings toward the
Masonic. follow it all the way there as the city activity gradually
falls away, the airplane slows, descends and finally lands gently on the
broad front steps. energetic photorealistic cinematic live action that
gradually becomes calm and poetic at the end.

## PARIS MOUSE

FOLLOW a little mouse as it scurries across a room and disappears
through a hole in the baseboard, then keep following it through all the
tiny hidden tunnels and corridors inside the walls of a building. the
mouse keeps running, climbing pipes, squeezing past moving gears,
jumping over gaps and startling insects and other tiny creatures along
the way. bits of dust fall, pipes drip, wires sway and the hidden spaces
feel busy and alive. keep climbing higher through this miniature world
until the mouse finds a way outside and races onto the rooftops
overlooking Paris, where pigeons take flight, laundry moves in the
breeze, chimneys smoke and the city bustles far below. stylized 3D
animated movie, warm and colorful, playful, cinematic and full of little
details.

## RUNAWAY PRAM

FOLLOW directly behind an empty baby pram that gets loose at the top of
a crazy steep San Francisco hill and starts rolling faster and faster
downhill, around 45 seconds, 6 hops. people notice it and react,
pedestrians jump out of the way, cars stream through intersections and
slam on their brakes, cyclists swerve, and it barely makes it through a
busy crosswalk. send it racing across the path of a moving cable car,
through street construction where workers dive aside and barriers
topple, and into more close calls as it keeps picking up speed. traffic,
crowds and the city should stay active around it rather than feeling
like a static background. build toward a big spectacular payoff at the
bottom of the hill. photorealistic cinematic action movie, realistic San
Francisco streets, exciting and a little funny.

## SYNTHWAVE CAR

FOLLOW directly behind a sleek black 1980s sports car racing crazy fast
through a neon synthwave world, around 45 seconds, 5 hops. keep the car
actively accelerating, drifting through turns and changing its distance
from us while glowing road markings and scenery rush past. start in a
neon city full of moving traffic, animated signs and palm trees whipping
in the wind, then race out through highways where other futuristic cars
streak past. continue into crazy geometric mountains with pulsing lights
and moving wireframe landscapes, then onto glowing grid roads where
luminous shapes and particles stream through the air. eventually reach
an impossible highway over the ocean where waves and neon reflections
race beneath us toward a glowing city. pure illustrated 1980s synthwave
animation, not realistic: bold black outlines, cel shading,
super-saturated cyan, magenta, purple and orange, wireframe mountains
and grids, huge striped sunsets, palm trees and retro arcade /
album-cover artwork.

## DIE-CAST RACER

FOLLOW directly behind a tiny die-cast toy race car speeding along a
bright orange plastic track, around 30 seconds, 5 hops. keep the little
car actively racing, fishtailing and bouncing slightly as the track
rushes beneath it. race through crazy twists and steep drops while toy
scenery, moving obstacles and other tiny cars flash past. whip around
tight banked turns, dodge swinging toy hazards and blast through a huge
vertical loop while miniature spectators and mechanical track features
move around us. then accelerate down a long final straightaway with
another toy car briefly challenging us before crossing the finish line
as flags wave and toy mechanisms spring into action. colorful playful
miniature toy world, cinematic macro photography, shallow depth of
field, realistic plastic and die-cast materials, high-energy
toy-commercial feeling.

## MEMORY LANE

POV taking a surreal walk down memory lane, but instead of a literal
street we're traveling physically through someone's memories and
subconscious. start somewhere recognizable and ordinary with people
going about everyday life, then let places, objects and fragments from
different periods begin moving and behaving strangely as we go deeper.
childhood spaces come alive with kids running past and toys moving on
their own, forgotten objects drift through rooms, old photographs
flutter into the air, and people from different memories appear briefly
in the distance before disappearing around corners. let memories overlap
and become increasingly dreamlike while still giving us physical routes
forward through doors, streets, hallways and impossible spaces. keep it
feeling like one continuous journey through a living mind rather than
disconnected dream scenes. nostalgic, uncanny, emotional and cinematic.

## SUNKEN PIRATE SHIP

POV dropping from the surface of the ocean into deep blue water and
descending toward a huge sunken pirate ship below. schools of fish
scatter around us, bubbles rise toward the surface and torn sails move
slowly in the current. swim toward the wreck and enter through an
opening in the hull, then keep moving through physically connected
flooded passages and chambers while fish dart through broken boards,
ropes sway, debris drifts and old objects roll with the current. pass
cannons and collapsed decks before discovering increasingly spectacular
rooms where coins tumble, chains move and strange sea creatures weave
through piles of brilliant gold and sparkling jewels. keep discovering
richer and stranger treasure chambers until reaching the hidden heart of
the wreck, where something unexpected is still moving among the
treasure. cinematic underwater realism, shafts of moving light,
suspended particles, ancient wood and a strong feeling of exploration.

## DITD --- MIND / BODY / ROAD

POV on a surreal journey through the mind and body, around 1 minute.
start moving down a long dark tunnel lined with old flickering TVs
showing fragments of strange 1980s-style music videos. move through
three different TV-filled sections: first glossy colorful new-wave
performances with dancers, geometric sets and bold pop-art motion, then
darker industrial post-punk performances with musicians, smoke, harsh
black-and-white lighting and analog distortion, then dreamy neon
synth-pop videos with performers moving through glowing grids, surreal
landscapes, magenta and cyan light and VHS effects. keep moving until
the TVs give way to a huge living network of neurons firing electrical
signals around us. travel through the neural world as pulses race from
cell to cell until it leads into a giant human eye where the iris
contracts and reflections move across its surface. pass through it and
emerge underground surrounded by dirt, writhing roots and insects moving
through the soil. push deeper until the earth somehow becomes the inside
of the body and enter a rushing bloodstream, racing alongside flowing
blood cells through branching veins and arteries. finally burst out onto
a highway filled with moving traffic and speed down a long open road
toward the horizon. make it feel like one impossible physically
continuous journey rather than separate scenes. dark, surreal, organic,
cinematic and slightly disturbing.

## SIMPLE MANSION --- SANITY CHECK

POV walking through an old abandoned mansion, around 30 seconds, 5 hops.
start in a dusty entrance hall where curtains move in a cold draft, dust
floats through shafts of light and an old chandelier gently sways. move
through a large ruined dining room where loose papers slide across the
table and candle flames flicker unexpectedly, then continue through a
narrow hallway as wall sconces sputter, shadows move and floorboards
shift under unseen weight. climb an old wooden staircase while rain taps
broken windows and leaves blow through the house, enter a decaying
upstairs bedroom where torn curtains billow and branches scrape against
the glass, then step through open balcony doors onto a balcony
overlooking a huge overgrown garden at sunset where birds rise from the
trees and vegetation moves in the wind. photorealistic cinematic live
action, atmospheric natural light, realistic materials, quiet and
mysterious.

## FIELD OF HANDS

POV pushing forward through a vast field where human hands and pairs of
arms keep emerging unpredictably from underground. as we move through
them, arms rise and fall at different moments and muddy fingers flex,
grasp and swipe toward the passing camera as if trying to seize it. more
hands erupt from the earth ahead and on both sides, dirt falls from
their skin and the field becomes increasingly dense until it feels like
there is no clear escape. keep physically pushing forward through the
moving hands rather than stopping to look at them. dark, unsettling,
photorealistic cinematic horror with dust, earth and constant grasping
motion.

# Patterns Worth Learning From These Examples

## Physical Routes Work

Strong journeys often contain obvious connective geometry:

**doorways, windows, stairs, tracks, roads, tunnels, corridors,
openings, caves, gates, paths, escalators, water passages.**

These give later filmmaking stages something physically traversable.

## Destinations Should Change the Journey

A useful destination is generally a meaningfully new place, world state,
scale, orientation, or story moment --- not merely a slightly different
composition.

## Moving Subjects Can Provide Narrative Propulsion

A squirrel, paper airplane, car, mouse, pram, skier or other subject can
pull the camera through the world.

The filmmaker does not necessarily need FOLLOW grammar to achieve this.

For example:

> POV chasing a red paper airplane...

can preserve an unembodied POV while using the airplane as a persistent
moving target.

## Grammar and Subject Behavior Are Different

> FOLLOW directly behind a car

and

> POV chasing a car just ahead of us

can produce very different camera behavior even though both involve
pursuing a car.

Do not automatically convert pursuit language into FOLLOW grammar.

## Style Should Remain Filmmaker Language

Useful style descriptions can be short:

> photorealistic cinematic live action

or highly specific when style is central:

> pure illustrated 1980s synthwave animation, bold black outlines, cel
> shading, saturated neon colors and wireframe landscapes

Do not replace an explicit filmmaker style with the default
photorealistic style.

## Activity Should Match Tone

Temporal vitality does not mean nonstop spectacle.

A horror journey may use reaching hands and moving shadows.

A documentary journey may use wind, wildlife and dripping water.

A whimsical journey may use active characters and playful environmental
reactions.

A quiet ending may deliberately reduce activity after an energetic
journey.

Preserve the filmmaker's intended energy.

## Preserve the FM Voice

These examples intentionally sound like things a filmmaker might type
quickly.

Do not require users to learn TunnelVision's internal terminology.

The system should do the translation.

# Core Principle

A TunnelVision journey should not feel like:

**a slideshow of environments connected by camera movement**

It should feel like:

**a living world being experienced continuously while moving through
it.**

**Destinations tell TunnelVision where to go.**

**Activity gives the movie something to happen along the way.**
